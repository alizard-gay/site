In the current day it can feel like the web is just a ton of robots, and tbh it likely is. On central social media there is a very high chance the person you're talking to isn't even human. Reddit has become just LLMs talking to eachother and sharing generated github repos.

But,

whilst building this site I've fallen down many many rabbitholes following web circles and badges and have been remembering how much I miss surfing the web.

Cross site linking

Whilst exploring I've found many methods static sites have to link to eachother.

The most common method are 88x31 web badges, those goofy little rectangles you see on people's sites.

These badges can serve a few purposes

  • Show used software
  • Link to friends
  • Communicate interests
  • Look cool

For exploring the uesful ones are the ones that link to other personal sites. I have a collection I'm building myself, that can help get you started.

Webrings

Webrings are a 90s and early 2000s concept that has recently become popular again on sites like Neocities. While not super common, they can be fun to follow when you find one. At it's core a webring is just a ton of sites with a common navbar containing a "previous" and "next" button making a full loop of sites.

Overall

This blog post really has no larger idea, I just wanted to show off some cool stuff I found. Exploring the "indieweb" been honestly really cool as there is a seemingly infinite amount of personal sites out there, each with their own style and weird bits. Ending off this blog I just wanna list some cool sites I found, longterm I cannot guarantee that these will not turn into something bad, so click at your own risk.

  • john.citrons.xyz: lists a ton of sites
  • alivetheory.net: cool art piece
  • bool.chat: Just a synced bool
  • psychpsyo.com: Cool personal site
  • webring.bucketfish.me: A webring
